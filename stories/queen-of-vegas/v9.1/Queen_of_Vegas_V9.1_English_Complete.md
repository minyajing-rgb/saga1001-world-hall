# QUEEN OF VEGAS
## SEASON ONE: THE FLOOR / THE GOLDEN CORRIDOR
**SAGA1001 Original Fiction | V9 Writers' Room Story Bible | November 5, 2026**  
**Status: Development and editorial review. This is a complete season architecture, not a finished English novel or a KDP submission.**

> **Series hook:** A landmark Las Vegas resort announces a $1.2 billion renewal. Everyone claims to be investing in the future. But what the investors, the family, the staff, and the people who live nearby hope to own—or keep—are not the same things. Some people run the business. Others run the game around it.
>
> **Evelyn:** A composed, warm, seemingly young woman of mixed cultural heritage takes a job as a guest-experience coordinator. She speaks with the ease of someone who has lived across borders, including in Asia-Pacific, but offers no résumé unless asked. People mistake her calm for innocence. Season One reveals neither her former rank nor the incident that caused her to leave the life she once knew.



> **November 7 continuation:** V9.1 includes a separate full scene-by-scene pilot draft, WHO OWNS TONIGHT. Evelyn's past, exact age, former job and any alleged incident remain unrevealed in Season One.

## 1. The dramatic promise

The series moves simultaneously through **four interlocking engines**:

**A — CAPITAL / THE GOLDEN CORRIDOR.** Real estate, entertainment venues, financing covenants, operating agreements, supplier contracts, and the long-term right to capture the flow of visitors through a city. The boardroom battle is not simply about who buys a casino; it is about who gets to control where the city's next dollars are spent.

**B — DYNASTY / WOMEN WHO RULE.** Margot, Charlotte and Vivian Vale represent different versions of power: inherited history, operational competence, and legal entitlement. They may agree publicly while privately disagreeing about ownership, responsibility, marriage, and what they owe the next generation. Their male counterparts are neither cardboard villains nor inevitable saviors.

**C — HUMAN / LOVE, SHAME AND DEBT.** An estranged marriage, an adult affair, an emotionally distant father, a compromised promise to a VIP, the widow who won't sell his diner, a daughter who wants her mother to retire. Relationships are not decorative. A private lie can compromise a business disclosure; a business decision can rupture a family.

**D — WITNESS / EVELYN'S DIARY.** Working a shift, respecting professional boundaries, walking through Downtown, watching the sunrise, sharing food, and listening. She can see patterns without insisting on taking charge. Her power lies partly in the freedom not to play every game.

**The depth rule:** Every significant decision must be seen from at least three heights: the capital or governing level, the operational/professional level, and the lives of employees, tenants, or residents. A banker calls an easement an option; a general manager calls it a contract; a diner owner calls it his front door.

**The episode engine:** Open with a disturbing result; show the normal day that continues around it; introduce a knowledge gap; force at least one consequential personal or professional choice; end because someone has changed allegiance, not because the writer has simply discovered another secret envelope.

**Tone:** Golden-black luxury, precise dialogue, an East-meets-West ensemble with U.S. appeal, credible operational texture. This is a drama of grown-up power and ordinary people living with its consequences. Avoid omnipotent protagonists, miracle technical hacking, casual misrepresentation of casino regulation, and reducing women to romantic rivals.

## 2. The public event and what it conceals

**Marigold Resort** is a fictional sixty-year-old Las Vegas destination. At a televised gala, management and an investment fund announce a proposed **$1.2 billion redevelopment**: hotel rooms, an entertainment theater, premium dining, some slot-floor modernization, and a new retail corridor. During the celebration, a major investor leaves without explanation. The media soon receives a cropped presentation slide suggesting the company is "selling the casino and dismissing long-serving employees." It mixes true elements with missing conditions and inflames distrust.

Above the floor, executives start emergency meetings. Below, dealers continue their shifts, guests lose their way, a supervisor changes the rota, and an elderly visitor asks where the old elevator has gone.

What is actually being negotiated emerges over eight episodes: a right of way connecting properties; future income from a venue; dining and entertainment exclusivity; operating and equipment contracts. **Real-estate title, management rights, and regulated gaming licenses are separate matters.** A transaction is not completed merely because investors announce it.

A public redevelopment presentation promises revival; a commercially sensitive side letter may tie a street-facing restaurant, a theater passage, and a supplier's exclusivity to the same beneficial economic interest. The CFO thinks of this as an ordinary financing accommodation. A family trustee thinks it may transfer choices that were never adequately discussed. The show's gray zone is the tension between defensible deals and badly disclosed conflicts—not a tutorial on how to break gaming rules.

The mystery is not simply "Who leaked the document?" At least three parties make different use of the same incomplete information: someone trying to halt the project, someone benefiting from a lower price, and someone eager for a scapegoat.

## 3. The power ladder

| Level | Power in public | Control in practice | Drama |
|---|---|---|---|
| Property and family trust | "This is our family's hotel" | Land, buildings, lease interests, beneficial rights | A beneficiary does not necessarily run the property |
| Capital and lenders | "We can save it" | Pricing, covenants, refinancing deadlines, exit options | The offer with the largest headline may carry the hardest conditions |
| Board and resort operator | "We keep it open" | Budgets, people, service and regulatory obligations | Attractive forecasts can hide obligations spanning years |
| Vendors and advisers | "We offer the best solution" | Cabinets, systems, live entertainment, branding, supply agreements | Personal connections can make a contract look conflicted |
| VIP, media, local economy | "We create demand" | Relationships, reputation, demand, visitors and foot traffic | The person creating headlines may lack decision-making authority |
| Compliance and supervision | "There are no shortcuts" | License suitability, game procedures, surveillance and controls | A procedural delay can be legitimate even when it is politically convenient |
| Evelyn | "I'm working my shift" | Listening, memory and freely earned trust | She could enter the fight, but doesn't have to |

**Credibility gate:** A VIP host cannot approve restricted financial transactions on a whim. Casino equipment approvals, maintenance and disposal have established processes. The transfer of property interests does not automatically transfer gaming licenses. An affair is not itself a regulatory violation; an undisclosed related-party business benefit can create an entirely different governance problem.

## 4. Ensemble: the person, the job, the private stake, the secret

### Evelyn — Guest Experience Coordinator
**Surface:** Graceful, warm, difficult to place in age, simply dressed, comfortable with several languages. She remembers details others forget. **Job:** Guest liaison, service escalation, cross-department coordination and proper handoffs. She cannot open gaming cabinets, access surveillance, override cage controls or authorize comp programs. **Private life:** A small rented apartment, tea, sunset walks, occasional dawn picnics, strange calls from overseas she does not answer. **Need:** To experience a day that belongs to her, rather than rescuing everybody else's. **Season turn:** She stays in Vegas at an ordinary job, without a triumphant promotion or an identity reveal.

### Margot Vale — The matriarch and former operator
She has ruled a resort and made difficult decisions herself. She loves the staff's history but knows nostalgia cannot pay the bills. Her daughter Charlotte wants autonomy; her relative Vivian demands an equal voice. Margot knows an old property agreement will matter during the redevelopment and dislikes admitting she once supported compromises resembling today's crisis. At the climax she must decide what is worth giving up to keep the family genuinely in control.

### Charlotte Vale — General Manager
A formidable operator, not merely a billionaire's daughter. She is fast, educated, and hard on budgets, yet reluctant to discard loyal employees. Her marriage to Adrian is publicly intact and privately fractured. She is unaware of every connection between his branding business and the theater/dining agreements she is asked to sign. In public she must manage a business; at home she needs to decide whether the image of a marriage still owns her. Her great choice is whether to disclose a relationship that could endanger her authority.

### Vivian Vale — Trustee and overlooked family power
For years others treated her interest as a symbolic inheritance. She understands that a vote is leverage, but it is not the same as running a resort. She has cared for an ill relative, feels excluded from major family decisions, and holds a historical contractual note relevant to redevelopment rights. She can use her vote to hurt the people who belittled her—or to write a condition that may cost her personally while protecting others.

### Victor Hale — Investment-fund partner
Courtesy is his sharpest weapon. He wants an economic interest in the theater, corridor and foot traffic more than a cinematic "casino takeover." His own limited partners pressure him, and his home life is no sanctuary. He can exploit the controversy around the deal without controlling every leak. The final trade-off is between extracting maximal contractual upside and preserving a reputation other people still want to trade with.

### Nathan Cross — Chief Financial Officer
Quiet, precise, devoted to solvency and covenant compliance. At home, his family knows him mostly as a promise postponed to next quarter. His models are defensible, yet some assumptions and side-letter effects were poorly communicated. His irreversible choice: disclose and correct a problematic presentation even if that ends his career at Marigold.

### Adrian Cole — Brand licensing entrepreneur, Charlotte's husband
Charismatic, commercially competent, tired of being seen as a family dependent. His marriage to Charlotte has quietly collapsed. He is involved with Sienna but has allowed each woman to believe a different story about commitments and dates. A licensing company linked to him appears in a side-letter beneficiary chain. The dramatic question is not whether desire is bad; it is whether he discloses a material business interest at the price of his deal, his marriage, and his image.

### Sienna Brooks — Entertainment producer
Glamorous and operationally exacting. She wants a year-round show that thrives without endless celebrity fees. She is involved with Adrian, but refuses to become the disposable "other woman" in somebody else's business drama. A venue schedule in her possession carries a timing discrepancy; she may have been used as a convenient messenger. Her choice is whether to speak accurately about what she knows even if it ends her contract.

### Miles Reed — VIP host
He can remember a customer's dog's birthday but keeps forgetting that his own son is waiting for him. A promised benefit to a prominent patron was discussed but not approved. His dilemma is whether to admit the limits of his authority, lose a major account, and finally show up for his child.

### Ana Ruiz — Night-shift guest-services supervisor
Blunt and fiercely competent. She manages staffing pressures and raises a daughter alone. She once moved available staff to patch a rota problem without falsifying records. She must decide whether to formally document a dangerous staffing situation when doing so invites examination of her own judgment.

### Rosa Ortiz — Veteran table-games dealer
A sharp sense of humor, exacting professional boundaries and a complicated relationship with her adult daughter, who wants her to retire somewhere quieter. Rosa knows old Vegas history but is no magic witness. In a workplace dispute, she insists on testifying only to what she personally saw, not what a preferred side wishes she had seen.

### Naomi Price — Cage supervisor
Rigid with financial controls; warm in her off-duty neighborhood choir. She has seen a colleague unfairly blamed after undocumented exceptions. A request for "practical flexibility" tests whether she will keep a clear audit trail even when executives find it inconvenient.

### Mateo Vega — Slot technology lead
A practical expert in cabinet maintenance, approved procedures, downtime and machine life. He and his brother disagree about a small family workshop. He is skeptical of promises that a new cabinet will magically transform floor economics. He rejects a shortcut and accepts blame for delays rather than risk an improper change.

### Jade Park — Performance analyst
Brilliant with data and clumsy with strangers, a musician after hours. Her model has repeatedly been selectively quoted. She wants decision makers to see both the upside case and the assumptions that could break it. Her own major act is publishing the caveats with the forecast.

### Leo Navarro — Vendor field engineer
A young, kind, funny professional determined not to trade on his father's connections. He respects operators and maintains a slow, unforced rapport with Evelyn. A sales manager wants him to overstate a package's likely performance. He and Mateo ultimately propose a properly testable alternative, even at personal financial cost.

### Jules Kim — Food and Beverage manager
Fast-moving, ambitious, secretly sings jazz at night. She wants profit and good food. She has received a preferred dining proposal and has not looked closely enough at its effect on local businesses. Her ambition is not punished merely because she has it; she must decide whether to own the consequences of how she pursued it.

### Ben Walsh — Downtown diner owner
Former dealer, blunt and loyal, widowed after years caring for his partner. His diner is home to night-shift workers. He has secretly entertained a buyout offer, so he is neither a pure victim nor a development opponent. When leverage finally comes, he chooses transparent negotiation over mythology about himself.

### Teresa Gant — Landlady and retired singer
Funny, intrusive, fond of dogs, and far less naive about property than Evelyn initially thinks. She once stayed in Vegas for love. She has profited from appreciating property values even while grieving displaced friends. The redevelopment asks whether she can defend the community beyond her own rental income.

### Omar Haddad — Security liaison
Quiet, principled, protective of surveillance privacy and his ill father. Reporters assume he has identified a leaker; in fact he has only a partial timeline. He will not turn security footage into an executive's personal weapon.

### Recurring voices
**Miguel Cruz, the driver:** A great storyteller with a daughter's tuition to pay, sometimes prone to an unnecessary detour. **Maya King, a young F&B worker:** Fascinated by celebrity life but unwilling to be disrespected in uniform. **Ellie Marsh, a retired nurse:** Attached to an old slot and the people who remember her, not deluded about guaranteed winnings.

## 5. Six longitudinal relationship engines

1. **Women and control:** Margot–Charlotte–Vivian represent history, performance and beneficial interest. Each public alliance costs private intimacy.
2. **Men and control:** Victor has capital, Nathan has financial information, and Adrian has venue relationships. They require each other without entirely trusting one another.
3. **Desire and duty:** Charlotte–Adrian–Sienna is about conflicting accounts of marriage, autonomy and contractual interest, not a simplistic contest for a man's affection.
4. **People who sell and people who run things:** Miles, Ana, Mateo, Jules and Naomi all create value but bear very different risks. Relationship-building and procedural integrity each have legitimate places.
5. **A city inhabited, not merely consumed:** Ben, Teresa, Miguel, Rosa and Ellie have identities beyond the casino. They can move, negotiate, prosper, struggle or stay.
6. **The quiet witness:** Evelyn gains the trust of people distributed across the social ladder. Being able to solve something does not give her a duty to control it.

## 6. Season One beat sheet — sixteen episodes

### ACT ONE — THE TOAST AND THE FIRST LIE

**EP01 / Who Owns Tonight.** A sixty-year gala announces the redevelopment. A prominent investor leaves abruptly; a cropped "sell the casino, fire the veterans" slide floods social feeds. Charlotte must demonstrate that financing remains credible, Margot refuses to be treated as having signed every condition, and Miles scrambles to preserve VIP optics. Downstairs, Ana alters her shift plan; Evelyn helps an old guest find a forgotten elevator rather than speculate. The leaked presentation contains a phrase absent from the board-approved version, yet one executive repeats it instantly. **Cost:** Workers lose planned time and investors lose trust. **End:** Two versions of the gala seating chart emerge.

**EP02 / Two Prices.** Victor's limited partners want a faster exit map; Margot's counsel demands the complete appendix. Nathan shows Charlotte a clean cash-flow model that understates the time horizon of some obligations. Jules receives an aggressive F&B target. Evelyn discovers that the Strip and Downtown are different cities when driver Miguel tells her who actually eats on each street. **Turn:** A second buyer is watching Victor do the difficult price-lowering work. **End:** Vivian receives a private meeting invitation.

**EP03 / The Old Seat.** Ellie photographs a relocation tag on her favorite aging slot machine. Vendor presentations promise an easy uplift while Mateo and Jade disagree about maintenance assumptions and actual guest behavior. Leo acknowledges that demonstration results are not guaranteed floor returns. Evelyn correctly documents the complaint and hands it to the relevant team; she never opens a machine. **Turn:** The proposed machine contract and the redevelopment project share a consultant. **End:** Adrian has worked with that consultant before.

**EP04 / The Upstairs Floor.** A patron tied loosely to Victor's circle expects VIP favors. Miles is tempted to promise more than he can approve and neglects another weekend with his son. Naomi insists on proper documentation and Charlotte faces the cost of comping rooms while the property needs revenue. Evelyn redirects an inappropriate personal request without making a scene. **Turn:** An unapproved verbal VIP promise becomes evidence in an investment publicity deck. **End:** A customer's name appears on a promotional list without permission.

### ACT TWO — FAMILY, MARRIAGE AND THE ASSET REALLY FOR SALE

**EP05 / Dinner for Eight Figures.** A prestige chef auditions for the new flagship dining contract while Ben serves a modest night-shift meal nearby. The proposed agreement turns the passage outside Ben's diner into exclusive commercial territory. Jules believes an ambitious concept could rescue margins and has not fully addressed the side letter. Ben has privately listened to relocation offers; he isn't simply the innocent holdout. Teresa tells Evelyn why people remain in Vegas for reasons that never appear on a financial model. **Turn:** Ben's own business partner wants to sell. **End:** Jules finds an Adrian-linked signature on the contract.

**EP06 / The Family Table.** The Vale family gathers in an expensive private room. Margot wants to preserve property choice, Charlotte wants operational credibility, Vivian insists she is more than a ceremonial trust beneficiary. Adrian arrives late; Charlotte realizes his involvement with Sienna intersects an entertainment agreement. Evelyn is elsewhere doing her job; the audience knows more than she does. **Turn:** Vivian knew an outside adviser had a separate economic connection. **End:** Charlotte asks Adrian what he has hidden that the board might learn first.

**EP07 / The Dealmakers.** An entertainment producer, a vendor, and a financing team each have a polished pitch and a deadline. Sienna takes a chance on a genuinely local act, threatening a celebrity-focused sponsor deck. Leo refuses to overpromise machine performance; Mateo questions an unrealistic rollout schedule. Evelyn takes a day off during a major trade convention, avoiding a name she recognizes from her Asia-Pacific past. **Turn:** Multiple versions of the same vendor bid circulate without evidence of hacking. **End:** Victor asks Nathan for the signable version, not the flattering one.

**EP08 / Not the Casino.** At the midpoint the resort model is broken into land, passage, theater, rooms and regulated gaming operations. Victor cares most about the path visitors must take—and the long-term economic interests attached to it. Charlotte sees why her seemingly triumphant financing plan could give away future options. Margot argues that guests are human beings, not arrows on a slide. Evelyn hears ordinary visitors describe the very same spaces as memories. **Turn:** The dining side letter and corridor deal benefit a connected party. **End:** That party has a private connection to the family.

### ACT THREE — WHO LIES, WHO OMITS

**EP09 / The Side Letter.** Nathan is interrupted at his son's school concert by an urgent governance question. He may have a legally defensible agreement but has failed to show how intersecting terms affect other stakeholders. Charlotte confronts Adrian. Sienna discovers there is a second license version she never saw. Evelyn listens at work and notices how frequently people say "help me" before explaining what they did. **Turn:** The earliest side-letter draft did not originate with Victor. **End:** A beneficiary's name is redacted.

**EP10 / Marriage, Inc.** Charlotte and Adrian pose for a public picture and finally speak honestly about separation at home. Sienna refuses both secrecy and a revenge narrative, though she might lose her contract. Miles must choose between a patron and his son's birthday. Leo invites Evelyn out to see the desert sky; she accepts on the condition that it isn't a work meeting. **Turn:** The affair makes headlines, but the lender pauses on an undisclosed contractual relationship, not a morality scandal. **End:** Victor's people had prepared a crisis-response timetable before the story broke.

**EP11 / The People Off Camera.** Ben sees himself labeled an anti-development organizer on television, even though he wants a workable lease. Ana argues for staffing safeguards. Jules coaches Maya, who still struggles with her mother's objections to night work. Teresa has to acknowledge her own financial interest as a landlord. Evelyn helps a colleague who once helped her, without giving a press conference. **Turn:** Ben offers a practical alternative site plan. **End:** Someone publishes a modified financial page as soon as his proposal is accepted.

**EP12 / The Second Leak.** A substantially more complete side letter circulates. The most convenient suspected leaker never had access to the new draft. Nathan is pressured into an individual statement; Vivian worries the family will be used as cover; Sienna refuses Adrian's attempt to align their stories. Evelyn's shift notes are used only through appropriate workplace handoff, not illegal surveillance. **Turn:** One person publicly opposing the project also has a right to buy after its price falls. **End:** Margot and Charlotte choose opposite positions in public for the first time.

### ACT FOUR — THE VOTE AND THE COST

**EP13 / Votes of Our Own.** Miguel asks whether his daughter should leave town for college while the Vale family debates another kind of departure. Vivian adds protections for beneficiaries and local stakeholders. Victor resists delay. Ben bargains honestly; Miles admits a problematic VIP promise and loses the account. Charlotte proposes that her separation from Adrian stop being a secret in business settings. Evelyn hears everyone's decision at lunch without becoming their judge. **Turn:** Even a decisive family vote can't override the lender's deadline. **End:** An alternative financing proposal arrives.

**EP14 / Forty-Eight Hours.** The countdown accelerates negotiations. Charlotte insists regulated approvals cannot be collapsed into a property agreement. Nathan admits a forecast weakness. Mateo refuses an improper machine rollout shortcut and Leo supports a testable, compliant alternative. Ana documents the shift risk in writing. Evelyn prepares for her weekend hike but helps an ordinary guest with a legitimate request. **Turn:** A seemingly certain guarantee disappears, revealing that the highest bid may be the least executable. **End:** Margot texts her daughter: "Could we talk tonight without being mother and daughter?"

**EP15 / Board Night.** Nobody slams a table. A conditional amendment changes the room. The family accepts a phased development, limits exclusivity, orders independent review of the side letter and accepts the need for the appropriate regulatory process. Victor gets some economic interest but less control than expected. Charlotte gains operational protection at the cost of marital and family trust; Vivian wins serious respect but no revenge fantasy; Nathan agrees to correct published assumptions. Evelyn helps a guest downstairs while the vote occurs upstairs. **Turn:** The adopted resolution is not the version the press has been discussing. **End:** One director retains an exit option elsewhere.

**EP16 / The Lights Stay On.** Housekeepers clear champagne remnants while the resort opens again. The transaction receives a meaningful but limited first-season resolution: a phased redevelopment path, an exposed conflict and further compliance work. Ben has a negotiation route; Miles begins repairing a relationship with his child; Sienna makes a smaller but more honest show; Margot and Charlotte are not suddenly reconciled. Evelyn stays at her ordinary job and hikes at sunrise with tea. **Final hook:** A new overseas name enters a property-related diagram. Several senior people recognize its significance. Evelyn has not seen it. Her story is not yet about revenge.

## 7. Three original dialogue scenes for adaptation

### Scene 1 — The gala / EP01
**INT. MARIGOLD PENTHOUSE GALA / GUEST SERVICES DESK — NIGHT**

Victor smiles into the lights. Charlotte stands beside him; Margot remains just behind, attending without surrendering ownership of the room. Downstairs, Evelyn is finding an old lift for a longtime guest.

**VICTOR:** We aren't selling the past. We're giving it a place that can keep earning.

**CHARLOTTE:** You promised not to say "earning" onstage.

**VICTOR:** I said opportunity.

**MARGOT (behind her glass):** Both your dictionaries cost more than mine.

A weary guest holds up a faded hotel card.

**GUEST:** This card used to take me to watch my wife sing. Does it open anything anymore?

**EVELYN:** Tell me about the show. I'll find the right floor first.

**GUEST:** They're selling the hotel tonight.

**EVELYN:** I haven't seen what anyone will sign. But your chair is still here. Come with me.

Applause above; the investor slips out; the live comments explode. A replacement shift rota is taped up downstairs.

### Scene 2 — The family dinner / EP06
**INT. PRIVATE DINING ROOM — NIGHT**

Three generations of portraits face the table. Adrian arrives seven minutes late. Vivian waits for everyone before picking up a fork.

**MARGOT:** Our first dinner together this year. Is it because of the hotel or because of me?

**CHARLOTTE:** Because you said everyone would come.

**VIVIAN:** You left something out. Everyone being here is different from everyone having a vote.

**ADRIAN:** I thought this was a family dinner.

**VIVIAN:** In this family, someone always remembers what they signed at dessert.

Sienna's work message flashes on Charlotte's screen.

**CHARLOTTE:** We can talk about you and her later. Why is your company named in that licensing agreement?

**ADRIAN:** I thought you knew.

**CHARLOTTE (quietly):** That is worse than telling me nothing.

For once Margot has nothing to say; she remembers a compromise of her own.

### Scene 3 — The asset map / EP08
**INT. INVESTMENT REVIEW ROOM — AFTERNOON**

The resort model lights up in four colors: venue red, corridor gold, hotel blue, regulated gaming purple.

**CHARLOTTE:** Everyone outside thinks you want to buy my casino.

**VICTOR:** I don't need your casino. I want the door every customer walks through.

**MARGOT:** Those are people, not lines on a presentation.

**VICTOR:** Which is precisely why we need your brand.

**NATHAN:** If that exclusivity goes into the deal, the forecast has to be rebuilt.

**CHARLOTTE:** Rebuild it.

**VICTOR:** Half the people who were going to sleep tonight won't.

**CHARLOTTE:** I want to know who gets what. That's worth a lost night.

## 9. Three-season promise

**S1 — THE FLOOR:** Life inside an operating resort; power above and below; a redevelopment reaches a qualified first-stage conclusion. Evelyn's past remains unspoken.

**S2 — THE HOUSE:** People from her Asia-Pacific life begin appearing as new contracts and ownership relationships bridge regions. Her prior position and difficult choices emerge gradually and from multiple perspectives. The hotel family confronts a second, more consequential inheritance question.

**S3 — THE QUEEN:** Only after new relationships are real does she face a decision: return to institutional power, pursue an old wrong, or build a different form of agency. The answer must be earned, not promised in the first ten minutes.

**Season One's last image:** People who voted yesterday report to work today. Evelyn walks down from the hills with hot tea and writes, "This city is very busy. I like the hour before it wakes." An Asia-Pacific name flickers across an asset diagram in a room far away. The audience sees it. She doesn't.

## 10. Visual system and production gate

- **Approved aesthetic direction:** Glamorous gold-and-black Vegas with limited blue-violet reflections, crisp faces and believable restrained expressions. Evelyn is a graceful American-facing mixed-heritage lead, not a dour CEO portrait. Mature powerful women and attractive young men belong in the same ensemble. No sudden face redesign between chapters.
- **17 image briefs for prologue plus 16 episodes:** a genuinely distinct narrative image per chapter, not sixteen variations of a glamorous woman at a roulette table. High-value anchors: gala, old slot, diner competition, family dinner, venue model, intimate public portrait, second leak, board vote and sunrise.

## COLD OPEN | 00:00–04:30 | A NIGHT THAT BELONGS TO NO ONE

### SCENE 01 — INT. MARIGOLD RESORT / CHARITY GALA — NIGHT

A sixtieth-anniversary celebration. Gold light fractures on the lid of a black grand piano. Waiters thread their way between the tables. Through the windows, Las Vegas glows with the steady pulse of an enormous machine. The screen above the stage reads **GOLDEN CORRIDOR — A NEW ERA FOR MARIGOLD**.

Charlotte Vale stands in the spotlight. Her husband, Adrian Cole, sits at the principal table with his wedding ring clearly visible and his company's exclusive champagne on display. Margot Vale watches from the front row. She has the calm of someone who has spent a lifetime paying for decisions she refuses to regret. Vivian, a family trustee, has been seated near the exit—not by accident.

**CHARLOTTE:** Tonight we're not putting our history on the market. We're investing in the next sixty years. Rooms, entertainment, dining, and selected areas of the gaming floor will be renewed in phases. Every applicable approval must come first.

Applause. Victor Hale, a fund partner with perfect manners, takes the stage.

**VICTOR:** History cannot live on nostalgia alone. What we are offering is the capital to keep a city worth remembering.

**MARGOT (to Vivian):** When did people learn to make collecting rent sound romantic?

**VIVIAN:** When somebody else started paying it.

Victor's phone lights up: **L.P.** He silences it. Across the room, an important investor abruptly departs without touching his champagne.

Before the applause can fade, dozens of screens flash to life. Someone has circulated a cropped document labelled **MARIGOLD GAMING DIVESTITURE / LEGACY WORKFORCE EXIT**. A breathless alert says the casino may be sold and long-serving employees cut.

Charlotte looks to Nathan, the CFO. He does not nod. Victor's smile barely moves. **CUT TO BLACK.**

## ACT ONE | 04:30–15:00 | THE HOUSE IS STILL OPEN

### SCENE 02 — EXT. HOTEL ENTRANCE / INT. MARIGOLD LOBBY — NIGHT

A taxi draws under the porte-cochère. A woman steps out in low-heeled dark shoes, a clean black blouse, and a well-used shoulder bag. She is pretty, calm, and dressed as if there is no contest she needs to win. Her employee badge reads **EVELYN**.

The driver, Miguel, has been describing two versions of Vegas: the Strip that tourists imagine, and the Downtown that belongs to people who work there. One restaurant sells a $30 dessert; another serves a $12 meal to people finishing at two in the morning.

**MIGUEL:** You're not from here. New people think every street is bright for the same reason. Some streets are bright because the businesses can still afford the electricity.

**EVELYN:** Is there somewhere the light isn't trying so hard?

**MIGUEL:** Head for the hills at sunrise. The city finally stops competing with you for the last word.

She pays without arguing over a small detour. At the entrance she makes room for a parent struggling with a stroller.

### SCENE 03 — INT. STAFF ENTRANCE / SHIFT BRIEFING — NIGHT

Ana Ruiz, the duty supervisor, is dispatching staff. Maya King is adjusting a brand-new badge. The resort is hosting a major gala, rooms are full, a show is ending, and regulars are still playing on the casino floor. Ana has no time for the presentation upstairs.

**ANA:** Front desk is short two people. The post-show crowd reaches the lobby before it reaches gaming. Keep guests out of the service corridor. Log the issue and hand decisions to the department that owns them.

**MAYA:** What if a VIP says he knows the chairman?

**ANA:** Then he should be especially good at using the front door.

Evelyn folds a paper floor map into her small notebook. She doesn't ask for an important assignment. She asks which accessible elevator is open tonight.

**ANA:** Why are you always the last person to ask the most ordinary question?

**EVELYN:** Because it would be nice if the guest actually got where they wanted to go.

### SCENE 04 — INT. OLD ELEVATOR / THEATER PASSAGE — NIGHT

An elderly guest stands beneath an unfamiliar sign, clutching a faded hotel card. His wife once sang at Marigold. Every wedding anniversary he returns to the same passage. Tonight, a display for the redevelopment has blocked the old route.

**GUEST:** They used to put my wife's name on that door. Whose name is there now?

**EVELYN:** The sign says Golden Corridor. Would you like to sit for a moment while I check which public passage is open?

**GUEST:** Will you tell me this is just how the company wants it?

**EVELYN:** I'll tell you the part I know. I'll ask about the part I don't.

She checks an approved guest route with concierge and venue operations, then accompanies him. He speaks of his wife's first disastrous set, unsold tickets, and eventual sold-out shows. Evelyn remembers the entrance but never promises the building will stay unchanged forever.

### SCENE 05 — INT. VIP SERVICES / OUTSIDE THE CAGE — NIGHT

Miles Reed slips into his best welcoming smile. A high-value guest demands that a benefit not yet approved be treated as active immediately. He drops Victor's name as if it were a signature.

**MILES:** Let me check the authorization on record. The best way to handle this tonight is—

**GUEST:** Just tell her who I am.

Evelyn looks at Miles. She neither rescues him theatrically nor embarrasses him.

**EVELYN:** Sir, I'll put you in touch with the person who can confirm the arrangement. You won't need to repeat your name.

Miles returns the matter to the authorized process. Naomi Price, who manages the cage, refuses to let a spoken promise change cash-handling or identity procedures. Later Miles opens a message to his former wife: **I may miss his performance again.** He deletes it without sending.

## ACT TWO | 15:00–26:00 | PROMISES ON PAPER

### SCENE 06 — INT. EXECUTIVE PREP ROOM — NIGHT

Victor and Nathan compare financing slides. The numbers involve room-refurbishment costs, venue revenues, dining projections, and a proposed cross-property passage—not a single inflated claim about the value of a roulette table. Charlotte asks about construction and people. Victor asks about rights to future cash flow.

**NATHAN:** The proposed price assumes improvement in operations. We also have to model room inventory being offline during renovation. I won't use the best Saturday of the year as an average month.

**VICTOR:** I don't want your favorite model. I want the one people can sign.

**CHARLOTTE:** Signing fast isn't the same as being able to pay. Which rights do you want—the property, the passage, or the operations?

**VICTOR:** I appreciate a woman who comes directly to the point.

**CHARLOTTE:** I wasn't flirting.

Outside, Margot is told that some older leases may require separate beneficiary consent. She doesn't interrupt the meeting.

### SCENE 07 — INT. THEATER REHEARSAL ROOM — NIGHT

Sienna Brooks corrects a lighting cue while Adrian talks about the expense of a new resident show. She knows exactly how much twenty dancers, a year of rehearsals, and a dark midweek house cost. She is much less successful at managing the feelings of the people involved.

**SIENNA:** A sold-out theater isn't the same as a profitable one. Where guests eat and walk after a show—that's what your people are really buying tonight.

**ADRIAN:** I need a branded restaurant next door. You need a show that sells on Tuesday without a celebrity in the headline.

**SIENNA:** What we both need are two clean contracts.

Adrian receives a text from Charlotte: **Don't make a promise on stage that I haven't signed.** Sienna sees it, offers no excuse, and returns to work.

### SCENE 08 — INT. VALE FAMILY LOUNGE — NIGHT

Vivian is reviewing the family trust's interests. Margot studies Charlotte, whom she habitually calls *the general manager* in public rather than *my daughter*.

**VIVIAN:** You're asking me to support a renewal. I want to know which decisions the family can still refuse once it's built.

**CHARLOTTE:** The land isn't about to walk away.

**VIVIAN:** Rights can. Put them in a side letter and someone else walks away with them.

**MARGOT:** A clever line is not a substitute for understanding the entire deal.

**VIVIAN:** And the story of how you saved this hotel forty years ago is not a substitute for my vote today.

For once, Charlotte does not speak on her mother's behalf. A clause on the table has been revised three times.

## ACT THREE | 26:00–36:00 | ANOTHER CITY, ANOTHER CLOCK

### SCENE 09 — INT. BEN'S DINER / DOWNTOWN — NIGHT, INTERCUT

The night menu is written in chalk. Rosa Ortiz, finally out of her dealer shoes, rests her feet beneath a chair. Teresa Gant, Evelyn's landlady, shares a piece of egg with her dog. They're arguing about a proposal to remake their street into a "premium dining destination."

**BEN:** Those presentations say the city deserves better restaurants. Tell me who wants a seventy-dollar plate at two in the morning.

**ROSA:** Some people pay seventy dollars so someone will unfold a napkin for them.

**TERESA:** A diner doesn't have to stay in the same spot forever. But people need a place to go. Don't make them grateful because you've moved them out.

An unsolicited buyout inquiry appears on Ben's phone. The figure is more than he expected. He puts the phone under his napkin and tops up Rosa's soup.

### SCENE 10 — INT. CASINO FLOOR — NIGHT

A row of modern machines is lit like a theater. Nearby, an older video-slot bank is quieter, almost domestic. Ellie Marsh sits beside her usual machine without playing. A sign says **EQUIPMENT RENEWAL REVIEW**.

**MATEO (to Leo):** A demo looks great at a trade show. Here we need to think about load, power, network, service access, downtime and a real floor return.

**LEO:** Then let's do a proper trial. Put parts, maintenance and downtime in the proposal. "New" isn't a synonym for "profitable."

**EVELYN (to Ellie):** Would you like me to find somewhere else to sit?

**ELLIE:** I'm waiting for a friend. She always sat here. Every time she won, I bought her cake.

Evelyn records an ordinary guest request for seating and access. She doesn't tell Ellie a new machine will make her happier; she asks the responsible department whether the seats are actually scheduled to move.

### SCENE 11 — INT. LOBBY / SERVICE DESK — NIGHT

The anniversary speech runs on the public screen. Ana reaches the desk as employees receive a secondhand image supposedly describing a workforce-exit plan. A frightened Maya snaps.

**MAYA:** Everyone is saying we're going to be fired. Why didn't you warn me?

**ANA:** Because I haven't received a personnel notice. If I tell you what I'm guessing, I'm the one frightening you.

**EVELYN:** Let's finish the shift we actually have. If somebody asks, give them what the supervisor has confirmed. We don't owe the whole building an invented answer.

Maya wanted the easy comfort of "you'll be fine." Evelyn doesn't give her a promise she cannot keep.

## ACT FOUR | 36:00–47:00 | ONE IMAGE, THREE USES

### SCENE 12 — INT. ANNIVERSARY GALA — NIGHT, BACK TO THE OPENING

Victor finishes his remarks. The investor is gone. An incomplete document is now the city's favorite rumor. A reporter asks whether Marigold has sold the casino. Charlotte's communications team requests the original version before commenting.

**CHARLOTTE (to Nathan):** Which version is in that picture?

**NATHAN:** Part of it is from a real presentation. The rest is missing. It is not an approved board resolution.

**MARGOT:** Then why did one of our people know exactly what to say within a second?

**VICTOR:** The market always hears the worst story first.

**VIVIAN:** Sometimes that's because the worst story makes a better negotiating price.

No one knows whether Vivian was speaking to anyone in particular.

### SCENE 13 — INT. EMERGENCY BOARD WORKING SESSION — LATE NIGHT

Charlotte insists that the public statement stick to verified facts: a proposed transaction; no completed transfer of regulated operations; no approved decision corresponding to the alleged blanket layoffs. She refuses to promise that there will never be staff reductions; that would be dishonest before the operating plan and board have been reviewed.

**NATHAN:** If we correct the revenue assumptions tonight, tomorrow's bank presentation has to be rebuilt.

**CHARLOTTE:** Rebuild it.

**VICTOR:** Do you know how many people will lose sleep over that?

**CHARLOTTE:** Then let them lose sleep over the real numbers.

Margot finally speaks.

**MARGOT:** At nine tomorrow, show me the passage rights and the venue lease separately. I want to know who can close the door, not whose name will look good over it.

Victor glances at his watch. For the first time, he has no immediate answer.

### SCENE 14 — INT. STAFF CORRIDOR — DAWN

The shift is nearly over. Miles sits alone, looking at a photo of his son's empty stage after the school show. Ana posts the next rota and leaves space for an employee who needs to pick up a child. Naomi signs out after making sure her handover is complete.

Evelyn closes her notebook. Her entry reads: **An old man found the way back to his wife's stage. A young woman thought her job was disappearing. A guest believed a name should be enough to change a rule.**

An overseas number calls. Evelyn watches it for two seconds and declines. Outside the staff entrance, the desert air feels cleaner than the conditioned air indoors.

Leo comes across the service courtyard with two coffees.

**LEO:** I bought one too many. Want it?

**EVELYN:** Thank you. Do you know somewhere to watch sunrise without a crowd?

**LEO:** Sunrise? You might be the only person in town not looking for a bed.

**EVELYN:** Maybe. I'd just like to see a light that doesn't have to work so hard.

He laughs, and does not ask who she used to be.

### SCENE 15 — INT. MARGOT'S PRIVATE SUITE — SAME TIME

Margot takes down a photograph from Marigold's opening. The original entrance is on the left. The new theater now occupies that ground. On the back someone wrote: **As long as the door can open, this city still has a place for us.**

Vivian sends a scan of a historical access agreement with a short note: **The version you approved and the version I received are not the same.**

Margot calls Charlotte.

**MARGOT:** At tomorrow's meeting, don't call me Mother.

### SCENE 16 — EXT. QUIET VEGAS STREET / PRE-DAWN — FINAL BEAT

Evelyn and Leo part ways. No invitation, no dramatic romance. She buys two rolls from a bakery just opening, saving one for Teresa. On the distant hotel billboard, **GOLDEN CORRIDOR** is still playing on a loop.

A brief insert: the same corridor is described with two different lease terms in separate negotiation drafts. Another insert: two seating plans for the gala exist, and the investor who left was placed beside Victor on the second version.

Evelyn rides toward the edge of town. Sunrise has not yet broken. She writes one final line in her notebook:

**I used to think everyone came here to test their luck. Now I wonder how many people are trying to buy luck itself.**

**CUT TO BLACK. END OF EPISODE ONE.**
